\section{Conclusion}

We formulate slides as low-frequency, persistent semantic evidence for
simultaneous speech translation: each stable slide is encoded once, cached
across its dwell window, and retrieved without blocking the audio policy. This
framing makes content specificity, temporal alignment, representation
sufficiency, and computation separately testable. Our current speech-input
probe does not yet establish local slide-content use, because a same-talk wrong
slide performs indistinguishably from the current slide. The next evidence must
therefore come from strong OCR and text-equivalent baselines, stale and
cross-talk controls, measured slide-to-speech timing, and cold/amortized/on-path
cost accounting rather than from another image-versus-none comparison.
